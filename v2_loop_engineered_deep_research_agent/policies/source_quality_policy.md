# Source Quality Policy

## Purpose

This policy ranks sources for M&A deep research and defines how source quality affects claim certification.

## Source Tiers

### Tier 1: Primary / Regulatory

Examples:

- securities filings
- annual reports and quarterly reports
- stock exchange announcements
- regulator publications
- merger agreements and offer documents
- official company investor materials
- audited financial statements

Default reliability: high.

### Tier 2: Company / Transaction Materials

Examples:

- official company websites
- product pages
- management presentations
- investor decks
- press releases
- board or special committee materials when provided by user

Default reliability: medium to high, depending on claim type.

### Tier 3: Market / Industry Sources

Examples:

- reputable industry research
- government statistics
- industry association data
- transaction databases
- market data providers
- analyst consensus\n
Default reliability: medium; can be high for narrow data claims when methodology is clear.

### Tier 4: News / Commentary

Examples:

- reputable news
- interviews
- market commentary
- analyst opinion pieces

Default reliability: medium to low. Suitable for context and market reaction, not standalone proof of material financial or legal claims.

### Tier 5: Unverified / Informal

Examples:

- rumors
- unsourced blogs
- social media
- unsupported third-party summaries
- model-generated statements

Default reliability: low. These sources cannot certify material claims without corroboration.

## Source Requirements By Claim Type

- factual claims: Tier 1 or Tier 2 preferred
- financial claims: Tier 1 required where available
- valuation claims: Tier 1/3 inputs plus disclosed assumptions
- transaction terms: Tier 1 transaction documents or user-provided materials
- market reaction: market data plus news context
- strategic judgment: multiple source types allowed, but dependencies must be explicit

## Conflict Handling

When sources conflict:

1. preserve both values or statements
2. identify source type and date
3. explain the likely reason for difference
4. prefer primary filings for historical facts
5. do not choose the value that best supports the desired recommendation
6. mark unresolved material conflicts as Needs Human Review

## Recency

Source recency matters. Older sources may still certify historical claims but should be caveated when used for current strategic or valuation claims.

## Missing Source Rule

If a material claim lacks source-backed evidence, it must be one of:

- Analyst estimate
- Assumption
- Pending diligence
- Not Certified
- Internal Trace Only

It must not be presented as a source-backed fact.
